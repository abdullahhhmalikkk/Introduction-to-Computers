## Introduction to Computers
A **computer** is an electronic device that accepts data as input, processes it according to a set of instructions, and produces meaningful output. Computers are used in almost every field today, including education, business, healthcare, and entertainment. At their core, all computers perform the same basic cycle: *input → process → output → storage*.


## Types of Computers
Computers can be classified based on their size, purpose, and processing power:

- **Supercomputers** – Extremely powerful machines used for complex scientific calculations (e.g., weather forecasting, nuclear simulations)
- **Mainframe Computers** – Large systems used by big organizations for bulk data processing (e.g., banks, airlines)
- **Minicomputers** – Mid-sized computers used by small businesses
- **Microcomputers (Personal Computers)** – Everyday computers used at home or work, such as desktops and laptops
- **Embedded Computers** – Small computer systems built into other devices (e.g., microwaves, cars)

| Type | Size | Typical Use |
|------|------|-------------|
| Supercomputer | Very Large | Scientific research |
| Mainframe | Large | Enterprise data processing |
| Microcomputer | Small | Personal/home use |
| Embedded | Very Small | Appliances & devices |


## History of Computers
The history of computers is generally divided into generations:

1. **First Generation (1940–1956)** – Used vacuum tubes; machines were huge and consumed a lot of power
2. **Second Generation (1956–1963)** – Used transistors, making computers smaller and more reliable
3. **Third Generation (1964–1971)** – Used integrated circuits (ICs)
4. **Fourth Generation (1971–present)** – Used microprocessors, giving rise to personal computers
5. **Fifth Generation (present and beyond)** – Based on artificial intelligence and advanced computing
